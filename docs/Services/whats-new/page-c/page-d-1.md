Damsons are small, oval plums with deep blue-purple skin and tart, astringent flesh, native to the region around Damascus — from which their name derives. Brought to Britain by the Romans, they thrived in English orchards for centuries and remain a beloved heritage fruit, particularly in the Lake District. Too sharp to eat raw with pleasure, damsons reveal their true character when cooked: transformed into rich jams, jellies, cheese, gin, and wine, their flavor deepens into something complex and distinctly plummy. High in vitamin C and antioxidants, they are nutritious as well as flavorful. Their fleeting autumn season makes each harvest feel especially precious.
