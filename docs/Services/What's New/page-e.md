Eggplants, known as aubergines in Europe, are glossy, deep-purple vegetables — botanically fruits — belonging to the nightshade family alongside tomatoes and peppers. Originating in South Asia, they have been cultivated for over 1,500 years and feature prominently in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and Asian cuisines. Their spongy flesh absorbs flavors exceptionally well, making them ideal for roasting, grilling, braising, and frying. Iconic dishes include Italian parmigiana, Greek moussaka, Turkish imam bayildi, and Indian baingan bharta. Despite their rich, meaty texture, eggplants are low in calories and contain beneficial antioxidants, particularly nasunin, found in their distinctive purple skin. White, striped, and slender Asian varieties also exist.
