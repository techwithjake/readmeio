Figs are among humanity's oldest cultivated fruits, with evidence of cultivation dating back 11,000 years in the Jordan Valley, predating even wheat and barley farming. Native to the Middle East and Mediterranean, they feature prominently in ancient Greek, Roman, and Biblical texts. Botanically unusual, what we eat is not a fruit but a syconium — an inverted flower cluster with tiny blossoms inside. Fresh figs are delicate, honeyed, and short-lived; dried figs keep for months and concentrate their natural sweetness. Rich in fiber, calcium, and potassium, they support digestion and bone health. Their flavor pairs beautifully with cheese, honey, prosciutto, and walnuts.
